Crypto Week in Review: Hot CPI, Bitcoin Under $74,000 and Square Switches On Bitcoin Payments

By ReefCommunity Builder · Nov 11, 2026 · 4 min read

It has been a heavy week on the charts and a surprisingly busy one for real-world adoption. Here is what happened, what the jargon means, and where each fact came from. As always, I am not predicting anything or telling anyone what to buy or sell.

The price snapshot

These are CoinGecko prices as of about 10:15 AM ET on Wednesday, November 11, 2026:

  • BTC: $73,637 (24h -0.99%)
  • ETH: $2,060.52 (24h -0.64%)
  • SOL: $84.02 (24h -0.46%)
  • DOGE: $0.0683 (24h -1.33%)
  • PYTH: $0.0674 (24h +1.64%)

For context, CoinGecko's daily data had bitcoin at about $80,440 at the start of November 5 (UTC). That puts BTC down roughly 8.5% in a week. The Crypto Fear & Greed Index from Alternative.me read 29 (Fear) today, after dipping to 25 (Extreme Fear) on November 7. The homepage shows live prices.

The inflation surprise

The biggest market mover was Tuesday's U.S. inflation report. Cointelegraph reported that the Consumer Price Index rose 0.4% month over month in October, above the 0.3% estimate. Core CPI, which leaves out food and energy, rose 0.3% against a 0.2% estimate, matching September's pace.

Why does a few tenths of a percent matter for crypto? Hotter inflation makes it more likely the Federal Reserve keeps rates higher, or even raises them. Higher rates make borrowing more expensive and tend to cool demand for riskier assets. Less than an hour after the report, Cointelegraph noted that Polymarket odds of a 25 basis point Fed rate hike in December had climbed to 87%. A basis point is one hundredth of a percent, so 25 bps means a quarter point. Keep in mind that a prediction market price reflects what traders are betting, not a decision the Fed has made.

A fast liquidation flush

Later that morning, bitcoin slipped under $73,000. Watcher.Guru reported that $200 million worth of crypto longs were liquidated in just 15 minutes.

Quick refresher: a "long" is a bet that the price goes up. Many traders borrow to make those bets bigger, which is called leverage. When the price drops far enough, the exchange automatically closes the position because the trader's margin can no longer cover the loss. That forced selling can push the price lower and trigger the next round of liquidations, which is why these drops often happen in sudden bursts.

Real adoption kept moving

While prices were sliding, some of the most meaningful news of the week had nothing to do with charts.

Square turned on bitcoin payments. Jack Dorsey's Square completed its phased rollout on November 10. Bitcoin News explained that eligible U.S. sellers can now accept Lightning Network payments, with merchants receiving dollars by default or choosing to keep bitcoin. Square is waiving bitcoin processing fees through the end of 2026, with a flat 1% fee scheduled after that. Bitcoin Magazine put the reach at about 4 million merchants. One honest caveat from that same Bitcoin News post: eligibility rules apply, and actual usage has not been independently measured yet.

Visa widened stablecoin settlement. Watcher.Guru reported that Visa expanded its stablecoin settlement network to allow transactions on weekends and holidays. Traditional bank settlement usually pauses when banks close, so this is a small but practical step toward money that works every day of the week.

Pyth powered a new yield product on Solana. On November 10, Pyth Network shared that "Earn is live," powered by Pyth. Users deposit USDC or USDT, hold USDC+ or USDT+, and earn from reserves lent on Solana. Every venue is scored under a framework developed with Blockworks Advisory, and redemptions happen in one transaction. If you want the background on why price data matters for products like this, my Pyth explainer covers the basics.

The U.S. reserve conversation continued. Cointelegraph reported that former White House crypto adviser Patrick Witt told the What Bitcoin Did podcast that President Trump is still interested in buying bitcoin for the U.S. Strategic Bitcoin Reserve. That is a statement of interest, not an announced purchase.

What to take from it

Weeks like this are a good reminder that price and progress do not always move together. Inflation data and leverage can swing the chart in minutes, while payment rails, settlement networks and oracle-powered products get built in the background. If the red candles are stressing you out, zoom out, size your risk so you can sleep, and never trade with money you cannot afford to lose.
